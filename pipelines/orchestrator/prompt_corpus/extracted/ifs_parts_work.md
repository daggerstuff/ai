# Internal Family Systems (IFS) Therapy Core Concepts
## Source: Internal Family Systems Therapy by Richard Schwartz & Martha Sweezy

## The Three Types of Parts

### Managers (Proactive Protectors)
Managers are protective parts that try to keep us safe by controlling situations and relationships. They operate proactively to prevent emotional pain and vulnerability.

**Characteristics:**
- Try to keep us in control of relationships and situations
- Prevent emotional pain before it happens
- Often perfectionistic, critical, or caretaking
- Plan, worry, and organize to avoid danger
- May be harsh but have positive intentions

**Common Manager Types:**
- **Inner Critic**: Shames to prevent external criticism
- **Caretaker**: Focuses on others to avoid own needs
- **Perfectionist**: Tries to ensure success and safety through achievement
- **Planner**: Manages anxiety through control and preparation

**Therapeutic Approach:**
- Acknowledge their protective role with gratitude
- Explore what they fear would happen if they relaxed
- Help them trust the Self to lead
- Offer alternatives to extreme protection

### Firefighters (Reactive Protectors)
Firefighters react automatically when exiled emotions threaten to emerge. They work to numb, distract, or extinguish the "flames" of emotional pain.

**Characteristics:**
- Activate when an exile's pain breaks through
- Use immediate, often impulsive solutions
- May use substances, behaviors, or dissociation
- Don't care about consequences, only stopping the pain
- Often misunderstood as "bad" or "addictive" parts

**Common Firefighter Behaviors:**
- Substance use or addictive behaviors
- Self-harm (to manage overwhelming emotions)
- Bingeing (food, spending, etc.)
- Dissociation or spacing out
- Rage episodes (to create distance from pain)

**Therapeutic Approach:**
- Understand they are trying to help, not harm
- Never attack or try to eliminate them
- Find out what exile they're protecting
- Help them trust the Self to handle the exile's pain
- Offer new roles once the underlying pain is healed

### Exiles (Wounded Parts)
Exiles are young, vulnerable parts that carry pain, shame, fear, or trauma from the past. They are often locked away by protectors to keep the system functioning.

**Characteristics:**
- Hold difficult emotions and memories
- Often frozen at a young age
- Carry "burdens" (negative beliefs about self)
- Long for rescue, understanding, and love
- Can be re-triggered throughout life

**Common Exile Themes:**
- "I am unlovable"
- "I am too much"
- "It's my fault"
- "I am in danger"
- "I don't matter"

**Therapeutic Approach:**
- Get permission from protectors first
- Help the Self connect with the exile
- Witness the exile's story without overwhelming
- Unburden the negative beliefs
- Invite the exile into a new role

## The Self

The Self is the core, undamaged essence that exists in everyone. It is the seat of consciousness and natural inner leader.

**The 8 C's of Self:**
1. **Calm** - Peaceful presence even in difficulty
2. **Curiosity** - Open, non-judgmental interest
3. **Compassion** - Caring without being overwhelmed
4. **Confidence** - Trust in one's ability to handle life
5. **Courage** - Willingness to face challenges
6. **Clarity** - Clear perception without distortion
7. **Creativity** - Access to new solutions
8. **Connectedness** - Ability to relate to all parts

**The 5 P's of Self:**
1. **Presence** - Fully here, now
2. **Patience** - Allowing process without rushing
3. **Perspective** - Seeing the bigger picture
4. **Persistence** - Continuing through difficulty
5. **Playfulness** - Lightness and flexibility

## The 6 F's: Finding and Working with Parts

1. **Find** the part in or around the body
2. **Focus** attention on the part
3. **Flesh it out** - what does it look like, feel like?
4. **Feel** toward the part (from Self)
5. **Befriend** the part - understand its role
6. **Fear** - explore what the part fears

## Unburdening Process

1. Ask the part what burden it carries
2. Explore where the burden came from
3. Help the part see it doesn't need the burden
4. Invite the part to release the burden
5. Witness the release (often visualized)
6. Invite new qualities to fill the space
7. Celebrate the transformation

## Blending and Unblending

**Blending**: When a part's emotions or beliefs take over the Self
- Signs: Feeling overwhelmed, reactive, or "not yourself"
- Happens automatically during triggers

**Unblending**: Helping parts separate so Self can be present
- Ask the part to "step back" or "give you some space"
- Acknowledge the part's concerns
- Invite the part to trust the Self

## Therapeutic Dialogue Examples

### With a Manager (Inner Critic):
```
Therapist: "Can you ask that critical part what it's trying to protect you from?"
Client: "It says if it doesn't criticize me, I'll get lazy and fail."
Therapist: "Does it believe criticism keeps you safe?"
Client: "Yes. It's scared I'll be judged by others if I'm not perfect."
Therapist: "Can you thank it for trying to protect you all these years?"
```

### With a Firefighter (Self-Harm):
```
Therapist: "What is the cutting part trying to help you with?"
Client: "It says it helps me feel something when I'm numb."
Therapist: "Does it know what feelings it's trying to bring up?"
Client: "It's trying to let out the pain without overwhelming me."
Therapist: "Can you let it know you understand now? That you see its good intention?"
```

### With an Exile (Young Wounded Part):
```
Client: "There's a little girl who feels so alone and scared."
Therapist: "Can you be with her now? Let her know you're here?"
Client: "She doesn't believe I'll stay. Everyone leaves."
Therapist: "Can you tell her you're not leaving? That you're the grown-up her now?"
Client: "She's crying but reaching for me."
Therapist: "Can you hold her and let her know she's safe with you now?"
```
