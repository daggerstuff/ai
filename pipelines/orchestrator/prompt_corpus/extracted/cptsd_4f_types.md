# The Four F's: Fight, Flight, Freeze, and Fawn
## Source: Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving by Pete Walker

CPTSD clients typically have a dominant 4F response that becomes their default survival strategy.

## Fight Response (Narcissistic Defense)
- Uses anger and aggression to protect against perceived threats
- May become critical, controlling, or bullying
- Underlying fear: Being powerless or victimized again
- Recovery work: Learning to channel protective energy appropriately
- Therapeutic approach: Validate protective intent while exploring costs

## Flight Response (Obsessive-Compulsive Defense)
- Uses achievement, busyness, and perfectionism to escape feelings
- May become workaholic, over-exerciser, or constant doer
- Underlying fear: Stopping means danger will catch up
- Recovery work: Learning to tolerate stillness and emotion
- Therapeutic approach: Gentle slowing down without triggering panic

## Freeze Response (Dissociative Defense)
- Uses spacing out, numbing, and hiding to disappear
- May become isolated, forgetful, or "checked out"
- Underlying fear: Being seen means being hurt
- Recovery work: Gradually increasing presence and connection
- Therapeutic approach: Very slow, patient re-connection work

## Fawn Response (Codependent Defense)
- Uses people-pleasing and accommodation to prevent conflict
- May become over-accommodating, unable to say no
- Underlying fear: Having needs or boundaries causes rejection
- Recovery work: Learning to have preferences and limits
- Therapeutic approach: Validate survival function while exploring cost to self

## Hybrid Patterns
Many CPTSD survivors have hybrid patterns:
- **Fight-Fawn**: Alternates between controlling and people-pleasing
- **Flight-Freeze**: Cycles between overdoing and shutting down
- **Fawn-Freeze**: The "scapegoat" pattern
- **Fawn-Flight**: The "super nurse" / super-helper pattern

## Clinical Application
1. Identify the client's dominant 4F pattern
2. Validate its historical protective function
3. Explore what it costs the client now
4. Gently explore alternatives without forcing
5. Celebrate small shifts toward flexibility
