The dimly lit room was thick with tension, the air heavy with the scent of stale coffee and worn leather. The client's chair was positioned in a way that made it seem like they were trying to put as much distance between themselves and the therapist as possible. The client, a young woman with a gaunt face and sunken eyes, sat with her arms wrapped tightly around herself, her knuckles white with tension. Her name was Sarah, and she had been referred to therapy by her psychiatrist due to concerns about her increasing isolation and suicidal ideation.

Therapist: (leaning forward slightly, trying to appear non-threatening) Sarah, thanks for coming in today. I know it can't be easy to talk about what's been going on. Can you tell me a bit about what brings you here?

Client: (her voice barely above a whisper, her eyes darting around the room) I don't know. I just feel like I'm... stuck. Like I'm drowning in my own head. (her hands begin to tremble, and she clenches them into fists)

Therapist: (noting the trembling hands, making a mental note to monitor for signs of anxiety) That sounds incredibly overwhelming. Can you tell me more about what's been going on that's making you feel this way?

Client: (her eyes welling up with tears) It's just... everything. My mom died a few months ago, and I'm still trying to deal with that. And my job is just so toxic... I feel like I'm just going through the motions, but I'm not really living. (she pauses, taking a deep breath) And sometimes I just get these... these thoughts. (she trails off, her eyes dropping to the floor)

Therapist: (internally: oh no, this is going to be a tough one... I need to be careful not to push her too hard) I'm so sorry to hear about your mom. Losing someone close to us can be really tough. Can you tell me more about these thoughts you're having?

Client: (her voice cracking) They're just... bad. I don't know if I can say them out loud. (she looks up, her eyes pleading) You won't think I'm crazy, will you?

Therapist: (reassuringly) Sarah, I'm here to listen and support you, not to judge you. Whatever you're thinking or feeling is valid. (internally: please don't let her be suicidal... I don't know if I can handle it if she is)

Client: (taking a deep breath) Okay... sometimes I think about hurting myself. Or others. (she says it in a rush, as if she's relieved to finally be saying it out loud)

Therapist: (caught off guard, internally: okay, stay calm... this is a high-risk situation) I'm here to support you, Sarah. Can you tell me more about these thoughts? What do they look like? (thinking to herself: I need to assess the level of risk here... is she actively suicidal?)

Client: (her voice flat) They're just... images, I guess. Me hurting someone, or myself. It's like my brain is just... wired wrong or something. (she looks down at her hands, which are still clenched into fists)

Therapist: (noting the flat affect, internally: this is a sign of dissociation... she's disconnecting from her emotions) Sarah, I want to make sure you're safe. Have you made any plans to hurt yourself or others?

Client: (shrugging) I don't know. Maybe. I don't know. (she looks up, her eyes empty) I just feel so... trapped.

Therapist: (internally: this is getting more and more concerning... I need to get her to agree to a safety plan) Okay, Sarah, I think we're going to need to work on a safety plan today. Can you commit to not hurting yourself or others until our next session?

Client: (scoffing) You think a piece of paper is going to make a difference? (she laughs, a harsh, mirthless sound)

Therapist: (firmly) Sarah, I'm not just talking about a piece of paper. I'm talking about a commitment to yourself to stay safe. Can you do that for me?

Client: (rolling her eyes) Fine. Whatever.

Therapist: (internally: that's not good enough... I need to get her to take this seriously) Sarah, I need you to understand that this is a serious conversation. If you're not willing to commit to staying safe, I'm going to have to consider other options, like hospitalization.

Client: (anger flashing across her face) You think I'm crazy, don't you? You think I need to be locked up.

Therapist: (calmly) Sarah, I'm just trying to keep you safe. That's my job as your therapist.

Client: (standing up, her eyes blazing) I don't need your help. I can take care of myself. (she storms out of the room, leaving the therapist feeling concerned and uncertain)

Therapist: (internally: that didn't go well... I need to document this session and consult with my supervisor ASAP) Okay, let's get back to the session... (realizing the client is gone) Oh no...

Therapist: (sighing) Okay, let's wrap up for today. I'll make sure to follow up with you soon, Sarah.

Client: (from the doorway) Don't bother.
