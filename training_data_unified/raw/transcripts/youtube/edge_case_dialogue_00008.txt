The dimly lit room was heavy with the scent of stale air and desperation. The client's chair was positioned in a way that made it seem as though they were trying to shrink away from the therapist, their body language screaming discomfort. The client, a young woman with a gaunt face and sunken eyes, fidgeted with the hem of her jeans, her fingers trembling as she avoided eye contact. The therapist, a seasoned professional with a calm demeanor, observed the client's distress with a mix of empathy and concern.

**Therapist:** (leaning forward slightly, voice gentle) Can you tell me what brings you here today? What's been going on that's causing you so much distress?

The client's eyes darted up, and for a moment, they locked gazes with the therapist before quickly looking away. Her voice was barely above a whisper.

**Client:** (nervously) I don't know if I can talk about it. It's just...it's been eating away at me.

The therapist's internal monologue noted the client's obvious anxiety and hesitation, a warning sign that the session might be more challenging than initially anticipated. They made a mental note to proceed with caution.

**Therapist:** (nodding) It's okay. You don't have to talk about anything you're not ready to. But I'm here to listen, and I want you to know that you're safe.

The client took a deep breath, and her voice cracked as she began to speak.

**Client:** (hesitantly) It started when I was a kid. My stepfather...he would come into my room at night. He'd touch me, and...and other things.

The therapist's expression remained neutral, but internally, they felt a surge of concern and a hint of anger on behalf of the client. They were aware of their own physical response—a slight tightening of the jaw—and made a conscious effort to relax.

**Therapist:** (maintaining a calm tone) I'm so sorry you're having to go through this. Can you tell me more about what happened?

The client's voice grew stronger, though her words were laced with pain and shame.

**Client:** (tearfully) He'd say it was our special secret. That I was his special girl. But it wasn't special. It was wrong. And it hurt.

The therapist's heart rate increased slightly as they listened, their mind racing with the implications of the client's words. They were aware of the need to maintain professional boundaries while still offering empathy.

**Therapist:** (softly) That must have been incredibly difficult for you. Did you ever try to tell anyone?

The client's laughter was bitter, and her eyes flashed with anger.

**Client:** (bitterly) Oh, I tried. My mom didn't believe me. She said I was making it up to get attention.

The therapist felt a pang of outrage on the client's behalf but kept their expression neutral.

**Therapist:** (calmly) I'm so sorry that happened. It sounds like you felt very alone.

The client nodded, her eyes welling up with tears.

**Client:** (desperate) I still feel alone. I feel like I'm going crazy.

The therapist leaned forward, their voice filled with empathy.

**Therapist:** You're not alone, and you're not going crazy. You're here, and I'm listening.

As the session progressed, the client's revelations became increasingly disturbing, detailing the abuse and its lasting impact on her life.

**Client:** (with growing agitation) Sometimes I see his face in my dreams. I wake up, and I feel his hands on me.

The therapist's internal monologue was a jumble of concern, empathy, and a growing sense of unease. They were aware of the client's escalating distress and the need to maintain a safe and supportive environment.

**Therapist:** (soothingly) I'm here. You're safe now.

The client's agitation continued to build.

**Client:** (frantically) You don't understand. It's not just the past. It's the present too. I've been having these...these urges.

The therapist's attention was piqued, their concern for the client's well-being and potential risk to others growing.

**Therapist:** (cautiously) Urges? Can you tell me more about that?

The client's voice dropped to a whisper, and their words sent a chill down the therapist's spine.

**Client:** (whispering) To hurt someone. To make them feel what I felt.

The therapist's heart was racing now, their mind racing with the implications and the need to assess the risk.

**Therapist:** (firmly but calmly) I need you to know that I'm here to help you, and we'll work through this together. But I also need to make sure that you're not a danger to yourself or others.

The client's gaze snapped up, their eyes locking onto the therapist's with a mixture of fear and desperation.

**Client:** (pleading) Please don't make me go back to that place. I won't survive it.

The therapist felt a surge of compassion and a deep sense of responsibility.

**Therapist:** (reassuringly) I'm not going to let that happen. We'll figure this out together.

**Client:** (exhausted) I feel...I feel a little better, I guess.

**Therapist:** (softly) That's okay. It's a lot to process. We'll continue this next session.
